# The bird

`kingfisher.png` is the common kingfisher (*Alcedo atthis*), from a hand-coloured
copper plate by **Heinrich von Kittlitz, 1832**, in *Kupfertafeln zur
Naturgeschichte der Vögel*. The plate is in the **public domain**; the scan comes
from Wikimedia Commons (`File:Alcedo atthis 1832.jpg`).

What was done to it, in this order:

1. **The paper was neutralised.** The scan is on aged cream stock; each channel
   was scaled so that a corner of the sheet becomes white.
2. **Converted to monochrome** with a mild autocontrast and a contrast lift, which
   is the look the plate had before it was hand-coloured.
3. **Dust removed — carefully.** An engraving is *made of* small dark marks, so
   "small blob" is not a test for dirt; a first attempt at despeckling deleted the
   stippling on the bird's breast and left white scratches through it. The test
   that works is the neighbourhood: a dark pixel whose 21×21 surroundings are
   less than 3.5 % ink is foxing on the paper, and a stipple dot is not. That
   removed 32 pixels out of 542,000.
4. **Cropped to the bird and padded** by 7 %, so nothing touches an edge.

The kingfisher was chosen as Rill's animal before this book existed: one precise
dive, straight to the water, which is what a language with no VM and a 33 KB
binary is trying to be — and a rill is the stream it dives into.
